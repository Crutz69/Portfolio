Sundmark World Builder

A procedural pipeline that turns a written design brief into a playable 8 × 8 km Nordic post-apocalyptic region in Unreal Engine, and a test facility where every gameplay system of the game can be tried in its own room.

Linus, game design and direction Built with AI coding assistants (Claude) under my direction Project: MySurvivalGame, a first-person survival RPG

What I did

Design, direction and review

I set the vision for the game (Fallout-style exploration and towns, Kenshi-style NPC life, people and vehicles on the roads) and designed the world it plays in: the regions, the towns and their roles, the road hierarchy, the catastrophe story and the look I was after.

The code was written by AI coding assistants working from my briefs, in many short iterations. My job was to decide what to build, judge every rendered result against the target, play it, reject what looked fake and set the rules the pipeline must follow, for example that country roads have no kerbs and every road ends on a road of the same or a higher class.

Every number on this page was measured in the engine. Where something is not done yet, it says so under Status.

  • World design documents: regions, towns, catastrophe, quality review
  • Rules for terrain, water, roads and bridges the generator checks
  • Review of every build from rendered shots and play
  • Planning: a demo-map plan that puts gameplay first

Case study 1

From a design brief to a playable region

The world starts as a JSON spec: coast, mountains, rivers, towns with their roles, the road network and a bomb crater with its damage field. A Python generator turns it into terrain and plans; Unreal builds the level from those files without anyone placing a single actor by hand.

  1. Write the spec

    Regions with threat and loot tiers, settlements, road classes, the catastrophe. Versioned with a changelog.

  2. Generate

    Python and NumPy: terrain, erosion, rivers and lakes, a least-cost road graph, town and village plans, dressing rules. About 8 minutes.

  3. Package

    Roads, bridges, building pads, kit parts and water as plain data files, with QA lints for grades, slopes and overlaps.

  4. Build the level

    Headless Unreal steps: a C++ landscape importer, water, vegetation, houses, AAA-kit towns, roads, dressing, bridges, navmesh.

  5. Review

    Rendered shots driven from Python over the editor's MCP server, audit scripts, then play in the editor.

Case study 1, continued

Cutting a playable demo out of the region

The full region is too heavy to play-test. A crop tool cuts a 2.5 km square around Kvarnvik and the farming village Fjällby out of the same data, keeps every world coordinate, and the same build steps produce a level you can play.

Debugging story

Why the NPCs could not leave town

The first navmesh looked complete, yet paths from the town square to half of the villages stopped after a few hundred metres. A flood fill over 50 m cells showed a clean diagonal cut through open countryside. Ruling things out one group at a time found the cause: the river surfaces split the navmesh into islands even with their collision off. With water excluded from navigation and real bridges placed on every crossing, all eleven villages connect.

Under the hood

Tools and techniques

World pipeline

  • Python, NumPy, SciPy and OpenCV for terrain, hydrology, road graphs and town plans
  • A C++ editor library for landscape import, instancing and spline meshes
  • Headless Unreal commandlets chained by a build script, each step checked by its report
  • Rendered review and editor control over Unreal's MCP server
  • Navmesh probes: flood fill, navmesh raycasts and path checks from Python

Gameplay in the test facility

  • C++ systems: teleport pads, pipe hazards and valves, fuse box, terminals and linked doors, lockpicking and signal-lock screens, puzzles
  • A 942-line Python builder for the whole level
  • Automated in-game probes per room and Unreal automation tests for locks and hacking
  • NPCs that climb ladders, use furniture and react to light
